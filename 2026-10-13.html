UNITE: A Modular Testbed for Reproducible Delayed Space Teleoperation

Dries Cardinaels, Thomas Pietrzak, Raf Ramakers, Kris Luyten (UHasselt / Inria Lille) · preprint, about 4 weeks old per Scholar (no venue or date given)

The authors reviewed 83 empirical studies of delayed human-in-the-loop mobile teleoperation and derived seven design requirements from them. They then built an open, modular Unity testbed with 14 modules covering uplink and downlink delay, input mapping, vehicle model, assistance, operator display and logging. The whole apparatus is a configuration file that can be shared. To test it, they rebuilt their own lunar study at 2.56 s round-trip delay. The rebuild exposed details that the original paper left unstated, such as how the delay was split between uplink and downlink and what disturbance parameters were used.

Why it matters: its point about under-reported delay setups also applies to tele-ultrasound latency studies. Reporting the delay split, jitter model and assistance settings would make comparisons with model-mediated approaches more credible.

Exploring delay and its impact on trust in teleoperated robots

Hamish M. Dawson, Theodoros Georgiou, Marta Romeo (Heriot-Watt) · BritCHI’26, 1–3 Nov 2026

The authors added controlled input latency to a robot used for a collaborative teleoperated task and measured how much participants trusted its performance. Latency mainly affected the operational side of trust: perceived reliability, responsiveness and controllability. This is a short HCI study, and the abstract does not give sample size or effect sizes.

Why it matters: a remote sonographer’s or patient’s trust under network delay is a human factor that could be measured alongside transparency in tele-ultrasound user studies.

Four-Dimensional Ultrasound Imaging of the Brain

Luuk Verhoef (PhD thesis, Erasmus MC; supervisors C. de Zeeuw, P. Kruizinga) · listed date 28 Oct 2026, full text under embargo until then

The thesis develops volumetric (3D) and 4D (3D plus time) functional ultrasound of cerebral blood flow. It includes a freehand 3D reconstruction method that uses existing intraoperative equipment, and a complete 4D acquisition system. The system was tested during neurosurgery and in awake participants, and the thesis describes this as the first demonstration of 4D functional ultrasound in humans.

Why it matters: it combines freehand 3D reconstruction with intraoperative neurosurgical imaging, which is close to your 3D ultrasound and brain-tumour surgery work.

Teleoperation & mixed reality

Mixed Reality Teleoperation of a Humanoid Robot: Implementation and Experimental Evaluation

B. Slominski, M. Hernandez, B. Domingues, T. Costa, D. de Souza, S. Melotti, D. Negri, L. Trabasso · XXVI Congresso Brasileiro de Automática (preprint), 5 Oct 2026

An HMD-based MR interface maps the operator’s upper-body motion to a Unitree G1 humanoid through inverse kinematics and streams the robot’s camera view back to the operator. It was tested on a bimanual pick-and-place task. The authors report that the lack of haptic feedback, limited visual perception and the robot’s mechanical limits reduced precision. This is a modest systems paper.

Computer vision, imaging & medical robotics

TRIDENT: intraoperative imaging device based on endogenous optical properties to evaluate bowel oxygenation in left-sided colorectal resections (first-in-human pilot)

Lapergola, Baratelli, Shein, Philouze, Kpekou, Pardo, Triponez, Mutter, Gioux, Diana · Surg Endosc, 12 Oct 2026 · PMID 42864469

The device maps tissue oxygen saturation without a contrast agent, using single-snapshot imaging of optical properties (SSOP). Ten patients were enrolled and imaging was completed in nine. Setup and imaging took about 4.5 min on average, workflow ratings were good or excellent, and there were no sterility breaches or anastomotic leaks. Surgeon-planned transection lines mostly fell within well-perfused tissue. This was a feasibility study only. Four authors are Intuitive employees and the corresponding author co-founded AstraNICE.

Why it matters: it is a label-free optical imaging alternative to ICG and hyperspectral perfusion imaging, and its industry ties suggest it may be integrated into robotic platforms.

Selectively Actuated Fiberbots for High-precision Minimally Invasive Surgery

Jinshi Zhao, …, Ferdinando Rodriguez y Baena, Burak Temelkuran (Imperial) · arXiv (cs.RO), 8 Oct 2026

The fibre robot is 1.9 mm in diameter and 1.2 m long, made by thermal drawing, and actuated electrothermally at its distal segment. It positions surgical laser fibres to better than 100 µm and passes through an endoscope’s working channel while moving independently of it. A phantom demonstration paired it with a da Vinci instrument.
