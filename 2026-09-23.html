Top picks

Digital Twin-Driven VR Teleoperation with Multi-View Spatial Perception for Surgical Robots

Chang Liu, Chenhao Yu, Honghao Zhao, Hao Ding, Haochen Wei, Adnan Munawar, Mathias Unberath, Peter Kazanzides (JHU) · arXiv 2609.25527, 22 Sep 2026

Builds a digital twin of the surgical scene from markerless perception and lets the surgeon teleoperate through a VR headset with multi-view rendering and motion parallax, using VR controllers. In a 15-participant study it cut path length by 86% and jerk by 95% vs. a HoloLens mixed-reality baseline, and matched or beat the standard surgical console on depth perception.

Why it matters: a direct VR-vs-MR comparison for teleoperation interfaces, which bears on your MR human-teleoperation design choices.

LiteSC: Lightweight Semantic Communication for Robust Wireless Telesurgical Video Transmission

Zexin Deng, Zhenhui Yuan, Xiang Ji, Yi Han, Gaofeng Li · arXiv 2609.23661, 20 Sep 2026

A semantic-communication codec for laparoscopic video over poor wireless channels. A frozen feature extractor cuts data dimensionality by about 97.9% vs. RGB, followed by joint source-channel coding. It beats H.265+FEC on SSIM and perceptual quality as the channel degrades, although H.265 keeps slightly higher PSNR in some conditions.

Why it matters: graceful-degradation video coding is directly relevant to streaming tele-ultrasound and MR video over lossy links.

Tracker-Free Robotic Ultrasound Calibration with a Spherical-Marker Phantom and Threshold-Free Center Localization

Kyoungmo Koo, Guangshen Ma, Xueding Wang, Mark Draelos (U. Michigan) · arXiv 2609.22483, 18 Sep 2026

Calibrates the probe to the robot flange using only robot kinematics and a spherical-marker phantom, with an automated sphere-center localization pipeline that needs no manual thresholds. Single-marker calibration reached 1.75±0.51 mm accuracy and 0.85±0.11 mm precision. Post-registration phantom reconstruction error was 0.41±0.35 mm, on par with more complex three-marker methods.

Why it matters: a simple probe calibration without an optical tracker, useful for robotic 3D ultrasound reconstruction set-ups.

Teleoperation & mixed reality

DITTO: Dexterous Interface for Transparent TeleOperation

Palacios, Lee, Zhang, He, Ciocarlie (Columbia) · arXiv 2609.19196, 16 Sep 2026

Pairs a 7-DOF robot hand with a kinematically equivalent motorized exoskeleton. The same device serves for handheld data collection and for bilateral teleoperation with joint-level force feedback. The authors validate it by training policies on contact-rich tasks.

MR-GLi: Mixed Reality-Based Gripper-Linked Overlays for Underwater Robot Arm Teleoperation via Bilateral Control

Sasago, Kobayashi, Uranishi · arXiv 2609.16041, 11 Sep 2026

An MR interface that anchors a reaction-torque indicator and the wrist-camera image to the robot gripper during bilateral underwater teleoperation. Summary is based on the arXiv listing abstract.

Case for Vehicle-Edge Collaborative Multi-Sensor Data Fusion for Autonomous Vehicle Teleoperation

Zhang, Gurumadaiah, Ye, Ramadan, Zhi-Li Zhang (Minnesota) · arXiv 2609.25304, 21 Sep 2026

SHARDED splits a feature-level sensor-fusion pipeline between the vehicle and the edge. The goal is to reduce 5G uplink bandwidth for teleoperation while keeping perception quality. Summary is based on the arXiv listing abstract.

Ultrasound & robotic ultrasound

Feasibility, Reliability, and Acceptability of a Haptically Enabled Robot for Teleoperated Echocardiography

Wright, Hidalgo, Roshan, Isaksson, Marwick · J Am Soc Echocardiogr, published 27 Jul 2026 (not previously reported) · doi:10.1016/j.echo.2026.06.013

Tested the HERTEC haptic tele-echo robot on 41 healthy volunteers against handheld ultrasound. Most standard views reached adequate quality, but apical views were weaker, and most measurements were comparable to handheld scans. Robotic exams took longer and gave slightly lower image quality, but participants reported high confidence in safety and good acceptance.

Why it matters: a clinical comparison point for tele-echo with haptic feedback, and apical views are again the hard case.

Computer vision & imaging

LEAP-NBV: Lightweight Edge Active-Perception for Foundation-Model Next-Best-View Planning

Hu, Ge, Krieger, Wang, Mohsenin (JHU) · arXiv 2609.23974, 21 Sep 2026

Distills large human-mesh-recovery (HMR) teachers into compact 32M-parameter students for on-device next-best-view planning on mobile robots. It is 2.0× faster and uses 3.0× less energy than the uncompressed models while keeping task performance.

Why it matters: edge-deployable HMR is relevant to patient body modeling for teleoperated or robotic ultrasound without offloading computation.
