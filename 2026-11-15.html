FDA public workshop and discussion paper: Evaluating Benefit-Risk in Robotic Medical Devices with Autonomous or Remote Teleoperation Capabilities

US FDA (CDRH), paper released 6 Nov 2026 · coverage: MedTech Dive, 9 Nov

The FDA wants feedback on terminology, benefit-risk factors and risk mitigation for remotely teleoperated and autonomous robotic devices. It describes the paper as a basis for discussion, not draft guidance. A hybrid workshop runs 2–3 Dec in person at White Oak and online. In-person registration closes 23 Nov, and virtual registration stays open. Workshop sessions include the current state of remote teleoperation, safe and effective teleoperation, and cybersecurity and infrastructure. MedTech Dive gives a comment deadline of 5 Jan. According to MedTech Dive, the FDA says no remotely teleoperated or fully autonomous robotic device had US marketing authorization as of July. The article also mentions companies developing remote ultrasound for technicians.

Why it matters: this is a direct opportunity to shape US regulatory language on remote teleoperation. Your human-teleoperation and tele-ultrasound work on latency, stability and transparency is relevant to it.

First-in-the-world Remote, Transatlantic Robotic-Guided Thrombectomy in a Perfused Human Cadaveric Model Using the Sentante Endovascular Robotic Platform

Grunwald IQ, Baltrūnas T, Clark S, … Hanel RA · Card Fail Rev, online Nov 2026 (Crossref record created 13 Nov)

Operators in Dundee and Florida performed 11 remote thrombectomies in three whole-body perfused cadavers, using commercial aspiration catheters and stent-retrievers after manual femoral access. All procedures achieved safe navigation and clot engagement. Median network round-trip time was 119 ms, with no disconnections or mechanical failures. Operators reported haptic feedback comparable to live procedures.

Why it matters: this provides a recent transatlantic latency figure with haptic feedback that you can cite next to telesurgery and tele-ultrasound benchmarks.

SURP-01: 5-Aminolevulinic Acid Fluorescence-Guided Surgery in Pediatric Brain Tumors: a Prospective Multicenter Phase II Trial

Schwake M, Bruns A-K, Krischke M, … Brentrup A, Stummer W · Neuro-Oncology 28(Suppl 4), online 13 Nov 2026 (meeting abstract)

The trial included 76 evaluable children and adolescents with contrast-enhancing intra-axial tumours. Serious (grade III–V) adverse events occurred in 23.7%, above the 18% target but under the 30% safety limit. Fluorescence was strongest in higher-grade tumours, and some low-grade tumours also fluoresced. Positive predictive value for tumour was 98.6% in the tumour bulk, 90.5% at the border and 70.8% in suspected infiltration zones. Fluorescent tissue left behind was associated with more residual tumour.

Why it matters: this comes from the Stummer group in Münster. Its finding that fluorescence is less reliable in low-grade and infiltrative tissue supports the motivation for hyperspectral quantification.

A scoping review of micro-ultrasound in male infertility: mapping the evidence from testicular microarchitecture to the distal seminal tract

Dell'Atti L · J Ultrasound, 13 Nov 2026 · DOI

The review included 9 of 91 screened sources, with cohorts of 7 to 94 men. Vessel diameter and density from ultrasound localisation microscopy were lower in non-obstructive than in obstructive azoospermia (AUC 0.81–0.95). No included study imaged the distal seminal tract or reported pregnancy outcomes. This is peripheral to your work: micro-US outside the prostate.

Subwavelength registration of skull-compensating acoustic holograms via nonlinear parametric array feedback

Dash PP et al. · arXiv, 11 Nov 2026

The method uses the parametric-array signal as a real-time alignment cue for transcranial hologram lenses. The authors report sub-wavelength registration accuracy, which they say beats standard neuronavigation. This is peripheral to your work: ultrasound registration for therapy rather than imaging.
