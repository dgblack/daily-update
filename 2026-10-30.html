Understanding User Adaptation to Network Impairments and Training: Evidence from Simulated VR Teleoperation

Gong Y, Zhang Y, Liu Y, Wu Y, Wang H · IEEE TVCG, online 29 Oct 2026 · PubMed

Thirteen participants did a pick–trace–place task in simulated VR teleoperation over three training days. Delay, jitter and packet loss were each applied at six severity levels. Gaussian mixture models sorted trial behaviour into three strategies: Continuous, Move-and-wait, and Predictive. Each impairment shifted the strategy mix in its own way, but practice consistently moved users towards Continuous control.

Why it matters: the strategy taxonomy is a ready-made framework for analysing how tele-US experts cope with network degradation, and for showing what model-mediated or predictive displays change.

In Situ vs. Monitor-Based: Evaluating Augmented Reality for Ultrasound-Guided Peripheral Venous Catheter Insertion

Souadda ZM, Olivares A, Zine Y, Drouin S · IEEE TVCG, online 27 Oct 2026 · PubMed

The AR interface anchors the ultrasound image under the probe and adds distance cues. Thirty clinicians (9 experts, 21 novices) each did ten insertions per condition in a VR setting. Experts took 524 s with AR versus 1126 s with the monitor, and novices 746 s versus 1165 s. For novices, spatial accuracy rose from 50.6% to 65.9%, and tissue perforations fell from about 29 to 3.

Why it matters: it adds clinical-user evidence that in-situ US display works, which supports showing the US image in situ in the HoloLens view for human tele-US.

Micro-ultrasound experience and clinically significant prostate cancer detection: a center-level analysis of the OPTIMUM randomized trial

Guer M, Lazarovich A, …, Ghai S, …, Klotz L, Kinnaird A · Prostate Cancer Prostatic Dis, online 28 Oct 2026 · PubMed

Logistic models with and without centre-experience proxies performed the same: csPCa sensitivity 0.851 and AUC 0.803 in both, with specificity 0.523 versus 0.544. Experience was not a significant predictor. Age, PSA, prostate volume and suspicious DRE were.

Why it matters: this counters the “steep learning curve” objection to micro-US. It is useful context for micro-US and SWE work.

Look to Move and Grasp: A Gaze-Only VR Framework for Robot Teleoperation

Wu X, Wang T, Zhang X · IEEE TVCG, online 29 Oct 2026 · PubMed

The framework offers three gaze-based locomotion methods and two gaze-based manipulation methods, controlled entirely hands-free from VR. According to the abstract, a user study found them usable and efficient. No numbers are given in the abstract.

Gaze-Assisted Supervisory Control for Lunar Robotic Arm Teleoperation Under Concurrent Monitoring

He J, Li Y, Runswick OR, Kamavuako EN, Jia Y, Hodkinson PD, Gao Y · arXiv (cs.RO), 29 Oct 2026

The operator selects a target by gaze and confirms it, and the robot then does a bounded autonomous grasp-and-place. In a 16-person VR study, time per rock fell from 47.7 s to 18.4 s and NASA-TLX workload dropped. Manual joystick control was still more accurate for precise placement.

Why it matters: it shows the classic supervisory-versus-direct trade-off in numbers, which is relevant to choosing how much autonomy to give tele-US.

Active Constraints for Simultaneous Guidance and Safety in Handheld Micromanipulation

Ho R, MacLachlan RA, Riviere CN (CMU) · arXiv (cs.RO), 27 Oct 2026

An optimisation-based active-constraint framework for handheld micromanipulators handles task guidance and safety together. It cut safety-violation rates by more than two orders of magnitude compared with baselines, while recovering most of the accuracy normally lost when safety alone is enforced.

Robotic Data Inoculation

Li Q, Zhou Z, Xu C, Levine S · arXiv (cs.RO), 28 Oct 2026

During teleoperated data collection, a residual policy deliberately perturbs the human’s commands to push them into states a learned policy is likely to visit. The operator’s corrections then become recovery demonstrations. On three real tasks, policy success matched or beat standard collection.

Why it matters: it is a shared-control approach to collecting teleoperation data, and could apply to learning autonomous scanning from tele-US sessions.

Visual Cue Interactions in AR-Guided Needle Insertion: A Prostate Biopsy-Inspired Phantom Study

Zou X, Liu M, Jones TT, Millan B, Gurram S, Pinto PA, Freidlin RZ, Martin-Gomez A · IEEE TVCG, online 27 Oct 2026 · PubMed

The study compared three AR cues: a focus-and-context window, colour proximity encoding and a trajectory overlay. Twenty-six users took part, seven of them clinical experts. The trajectory overlay helped novices most, while experts benefited more from the window and colour cues, so the best cue depends on expertise.

SimRetract: Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Robotic Assistance for Retractor Take-Over in Open Surgery

Wang X, Yang Z, Kim SE, …, Rajpurkar P · arXiv (cs.RO), 28 Oct 2026

A robot arm trained only in simulation takes a retractor from the surgeon’s hand and holds retraction. In phantom trials it grasped the retractor 90% of the time and kept retraction in 78% of cases. Removing the instrument mask sharply degraded real-robot performance.

Computer vision & imaging

Common Offset Gathers for Efficient Multistatic Beamforming in Frequency Domain

Jakovljevic M, Wang X, Candel I, Samir AE, Thomenius K · arXiv, 29 Oct 2026

The method borrows common-offset gathers from seismic imaging for frequency-domain ultrasound beamforming, processing one offset gather at a time. It reconstructs faster than delay-and-sum and saves large amounts of memory and energy on matrix arrays.

Why it matters: cheaper volumetric beamforming for matrix probes would make portable 3D US more practical.
