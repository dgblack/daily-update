Top picks

Cognitive and Behavioural Effects of Haptic Feedback for ROV Teleoperation in Hazardous Environments

Brown J, Farkhatdinov I, Jenkin M · arXiv (cs.RO), 4 Nov 2026 · accepted to IEEE Telepresence 2026

In-water trials tested a soft vibrating touchpad that signals an unseen cross-current to ROV pilots. Haptic cues lowered physical and temporal demand and improved self-rated performance, and pilots drove significantly faster. The same condition gave the highest mental demand and frustration scores. The abstract does not give sample sizes.

Why it matters: it shows haptic cues trading lower physical effort for higher cognitive load, a tradeoff that also applies to force or guidance cues for a human follower in tele-ultrasound.

Accuracy of intraoperative image-free and tracker-free mixed reality-guided pedicle screw placement across the entire spine: a cadaveric study

Ohashi M, Sato M, Tashi H, Shibuya Y, Minato K, et al. · J Clin Neurosci 155:112374, online 4 Nov 2026 · PubMed 42981639

A Magic Leap One headset showed holographic trajectory lines from preoperative CT, with no intraoperative imaging or external tracker. Across 8 cadavers, 261 screws were placed from C2 to L5. By Gertzbein–Robbins grading, 97.3% were clinically acceptable: 93.5% cervical, 99.2% thoracic and 100% lumbar. One experienced surgeon placed every screw, which the authors say limits how far the results generalize.

Why it matters: it shows registration-light headset guidance can reach clinically useful accuracy, which is relevant to MR overlays for a remote operator.

Nested Field-of-View Barriers for Teleoperated Multi-Robot Relay Chains

Vangasse A, Cruz T, Soares T, Cid A, Gonçalves V, Freitas G, Pimenta L · arXiv (cs.RO), 3 Nov 2026

In communication-denied areas, autonomous relay robots follow a teleoperated leader and keep line of sight back to the base station. A strict field-of-view control barrier function guarantees connectivity. A contracted, soft field of view pulls followers inward early, so the leader is not stalled. The authors prove QP feasibility and forward invariance, and in Crazyflie software-in-the-loop simulations the links stay closer to the connected set.

Teleoperation, haptics & mixed reality

Haptic Shared Control for Multi-Robot Systems

Bernard M · arXiv (PhD thesis), 3 Nov 2026

This thesis studies haptic shared control of multi-robot groups under three supervision modes: single-agent, pinning and centroid. It includes a user study comparing performance with and without haptic cues.

Mediating Human-Swarm Interactions Through a Robot Helper: A Shepherding-Inspired Approach

Yadav A, Jörg S, Jain E · arXiv, 2 Nov 2026 · IROS 2026

In a VR shepherding task, operators either teleoperated an intermediary robot directly or used higher-level motion primitives. Primitives that act on the swarm’s collective shape, such as boundary-based control, improved performance and lowered perceived workload. The abstract gives no numbers.

Selfrionette: A Fingertip Force-Input Controller for Continuous Full-Body Avatar Manipulation and Diverse Haptic Interactions

Hashimoto T, Hirao Y · arXiv, 2 Nov 2026

Fingertip forces drive a VR avatar’s whole-body movement and walking, and the fixed fingers provide counterforce haptics. In user studies, haptic realism was comparable to body tracking in confined spaces.

TRAILS: Trajectory Generation for Automated Indoor Locomotion Studies

Kalliokoski J, Center EG, LaValle SM, Ojala T, Sakcak B · arXiv, 2 Nov 2026 · IEEE Telepresence 2026

TRAILS is an open, seed-controlled generator of indoor VR layouts, Bézier paths and speed profiles. It produces navigation trials of equal duration that vary in geometry, wall proximity and velocity, for telepresence and human-subject experiments.

Remote health & training

Utilising wearable technology to enhance neurosurgical training: a systematic scoping review

Zhu H, Booker J, Aapan S, Cheema U, Starup-Hansen J, et al. · Br J Neurosurg, online 2 Nov 2026 · PubMed 42969069

The review covers 41 studies with 985 participants, with searches up to May 2024. Head-mounted displays were used mainly for AR and MR, and HTC VIVE and HoloLens use was associated with better performance. Smart glasses were used for telementoring and recording. The main barriers were learning curves, technical issues and discomfort.
