A lightweight portable ultrasound robot for autonomous liver sonography

Li Z, Xu Y, Zhang L, Han T, Yang X, Wang Y, …, Liao H, Ning G · Nature Communications, listed by Scholar about 7 days ago · arXiv preprint 2510.08106

The system is a 588 g, 6-DoF, cable-driven robot that straps onto the patient’s abdomen and moves with them. An AI agent drives it to acquire standard liver planes autonomously. According to the preprint, the planes were expert-level and pathology was detected in patients. These included two patients in high-altitude Xining (2,261 m), people in rapid motion, and scans in wilderness settings. The Nature page would not open for me, so this summary comes from the arXiv abstract.

Why it matters: it is a strong competitor framing for tele-US in remote settings. A body-mounted lightweight robot sidesteps the patient-motion and portability problems that human teleoperation and large arm-based systems face.

Robotic Bronchoscopy Delivery of Optical Ultrasound Imaging for Transbronchial Lung Biopsy Guidance

Zhang S, Bodian S, Nassren N, Hooi AK, …, Desjardins AE, Alles EJ (UCL, UCLH, UBC) · Frontiers in Medical Technology, accepted 27 Oct 2026 (provisional)

A side-viewing optical-ultrasound probe was passed through a clinical robotic-bronchoscopy catheter. The probe is 1.8 mm across, over 2 m long and images in real time at 4 Hz. In a realistic lung phantom it showed an inclusion from inside the airway, and the needle trajectory was visible in bench tests. Catheter navigation was not visibly impaired. The probe’s estimated failure bend radius is about 5 mm. Testing so far is phantom-only.

Why it matters: it is a UBC co-authored step toward image-guided robotic interventions using all-optical US, which is relevant if your group is looking at miniature US for robotic tools.
