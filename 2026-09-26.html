Top picks

Stabilizing telerobotic endobronchial imaging and interventions in breathing lungs with a helical brace

Zhang, Yuan, Xu, …, Dupont, Ren, Yuan (CUHK; Boston Children's/Harvard) · Science Robotics, epub 23 Sep 2026

A reconfigurable helical brace is built from stacked tubular modules, and its diameter can be tuned by almost 10× (3.0 to 29.3 mm). It anchors a telerobotic catheter inside the airway. In phantoms it cut breathing-induced catheter displacement by about 96% while keeping more than 97.4% of airflow conductance. Animal studies showed targeted drug delivery and tissue sampling with submillimetre precision, with optical imaging quality preserved. (Summary is based on the PubMed abstract.)

Why it matters: the brace stabilises the target mechanically rather than compensating for motion in the teleoperation controller, which is an alternative to model-based handling of breathing motion.

Adversarial Domain Adaptation for False-Positive Reduction in Hospital-to-Robotic Echocardiographic Screening Under Limited Target-Domain Data

Hsu Thiri Soe (Waseda University) · Frontiers in Robotics and AI, accepted 18 Sep 2026

A wall-motion-abnormality detector trained on hospital echo lost specificity when applied to robot-acquired scans, dropping from 90.6% to 65.7%, even though signal-to-noise ratio was similar. The author attributes the drop mainly to differences in acquisition geometry. A 3D ResNet-18 with gradient-reversal adversarial adaptation restored specificity to 89.3%, recovering 94.8% of the lost performance. It needed no labelled pathological cases acquired by the robot.

Why it matters: this is a concrete measurement of the domain gap between images a robot acquires and images a sonographer acquires. Any AI that runs on top of tele-ultrasound or robotic scanning will face it.

Telerobotic ultrasound assessment of acute sport-related Achilles tendon ruptures and gastrocnemius muscle tears: a clinical feasibility study

Liu, Zhao, Luo, Jiang, Niu, Cui (Peking University Third Hospital) · J Sci Med Sport, epub 19 Aug 2026 (older than a week, but not covered before)

A prospective study of 61 participants: 12 with acute injuries and 49 healthy. Telerobotic and conventional ultrasound identified exactly the same positive cases. Image-quality concordance was 84.4%, and telerobotic exams took substantially longer. 70.5% of participants reported high satisfaction. Physicians said they were willing to use the robot routinely in 91.8% of exams.

Why it matters: this is clinical evidence for tele-ultrasound in musculoskeletal work, and its exam-time penalty is a baseline to compare human teleoperation against.

Ultrasound & robotic ultrasound

Application of 3D Ultrasound Imaging in School-based Screening for Scoliosis: A Prospective Cross-sectional Study

Shen, Ding, Liu, Wang, Yin, Han, Wang · Research Square preprint, 23 Sep 2026

A tiered screening of 9,128 students: a scoliometer exam first, then 3D ultrasound, then X-ray to confirm. Ultrasound angles correlated with X-ray at r = 0.91. With a 10° threshold, ultrasound had 89.5% sensitivity and cut X-ray referrals by about 26%.

Why it matters: a large-scale deployment of 3D ultrasound reconstruction in the field.

Computer vision & imaging

Endoscopic multispectral imaging sensor systems for intraoperative tissue differentiation

Rüdinger, Holstein, Tarnoruzki, Lösch, Haist, Reichelt (University of Stuttgart, ITO) · J Biomed Optics 31(9), 22 Sep 2026

The authors measured hyperspectral signatures from 450 to 1390 nm in healthy and malignant breast tissue. From these they chose a few discriminative channels and built two miniaturised sensors, each with a 90° field of view, that fit commercial endoscopes. Transmission in the SWIR range was a limitation. The goal is real-time assessment of tumour margins during surgery.

Why it matters: selecting a few spectral bands to get real-time hyperspectral imaging parallels the band-selection problem in fluorescence-guided brain surgery.
