Top picks

LocoPilot: Single-Operator Bilateral Teleoperation for Loco-Manipulation Policy Learning

Antonio Langella, Giovanni Minelli, Giovanni B. Dessy, Lorenzo Amatucci, Giulio Turrisi, Victor Barasuol, Claudio Semini (IIT) · arXiv (cs.RO), 20 Oct 2026

A second Piper arm acts as the leader for the follower arm on a Unitree Go2, and handheld controllers drive the base and body posture. Interaction forces on the follower are estimated without a force/torque sensor and returned to the operator through a passivity-constrained haptic channel. A unified 11-D action is shared between teleoperation and deployment, so a fine-tuned MolmoAct2 policy runs without retargeting or extra IK. The abstract gives no quantitative results.

Why it matters: it is a low-cost, sensorless example of bilateral force feedback with passivity enforcement, the same problem as haptic tele-ultrasound without a probe F/T sensor.

What Novice Operators See Changes What the Policy Learns: A User Study of Keypoint Hand Visual Representation for Teleoperated Demonstration

Amane Yamaguchi, Shohei Kamiguchi, Masashi Hamaya, Shigeo Yoshida, Takuji Narumi, Mai Nishimura · arXiv (cs.RO/cs.HC), 20 Oct 2026

PointHand renders a multi-fingered robot hand as sparse keypoints. Eighteen novices and four experts ran 2,200 simulated mug-in-box trials under three renderings. Emphasising only the thumb and index finger gave 441 successes from 600 trials, against 320 with the real hand shape. Those demonstrations were the worst for a point-cloud policy (46.3%) but the best for a keypoint policy (57.3%, +11.0 pp across all five seeds).

Why it matters: it gives evidence that the visual cue shown to the operator, such as an MR overlay or a ghost probe, shapes both human performance and the value of the logged data. This is relevant if human-teleoperation sessions are to double as training data.

Compositional Humanoid Manipulation through Isomorphic Teleoperation and Independent Skill Routing

Yiming Wang, Peixian Yang, Lifeng Zhuo, et al., Daolin Ma · arXiv (cs.RO), 19 Oct 2026

G1Exo is an isomorphic exoskeleton that gives joint-level control of a Unitree G1’s arms for contact-rich demonstrations. HS-MoDP, a semantic mixture-of-experts diffusion policy, composes separately demonstrated arm skills. Switching the demonstrations from end-effector VR-IK to the exoskeleton raised full cabinet-workflow success from 35% to 65%.

Why it matters: it is a clean comparison of a joint-space physical interface against VR hand tracking for contact tasks.

Teleoperation & mixed reality

CAESAR: Mixed Reality Search-and-Rescue Platform for Cognitive Attack Evaluation

Mingyu Zhu, Jiangong Chen, Yu Liu, et al., Bin Li · arXiv (cs.HC), 20 Oct 2026 · ISMAR 2026 / TVCG special issue

CAESAR is a multi-user MR testbed that injects four classes of “cognitive attack” into a shared scenario. These attacks distort visual feedback, virtual content or synchronisation. A controlled user study of a latency-injection attack found measurable effects on performance and behaviour. The abstract gives no participant count or numbers.

Why it matters: it is relevant to security and robustness arguments for networked MR tele-ultrasound, where shared-state latency is the attack surface.

SplatXR: A Toolkit for Multi-Layered Extended Reality via On-Headset 3D Gaussian Splatting

Cyrus Vachha, Mohamed Kari, Parastoo Abtahi · arXiv (cs.HC), 19 Oct 2026 · UIST 2026

SplatXR is a Unity toolkit for building interactive XR apps on Gaussian splats. Its building blocks are a standalone renderer, splat-to-mesh reconstruction, splat physics, blending, and on-headset object capture with offloaded reconstruction. It is validated with demo apps and a small developer study.

Why it matters: it is a possible route to photoreal remote-scene rendering, such as the patient or room, for the expert side of MR teleoperation.

Ultrasound & robotic ultrasound

MoWE: Spatially Adaptive Mixture-of-Wavelet-Experts Pooling for Ultrasound Lesion Segmentation

Reaz Hassan Joarder, Sidratul Tanzila Tasmi, Alif Arshad Bakshi, Md. Bakhtiar Hasan, Md Hasanul Kabir · arXiv (cs.CV), 18 Oct 2026 · WACV 2027

MoWE replaces U-Net pooling and strided convolutions with a router that assigns each region to a wavelet family, which preserves boundary detail in speckled images. On BUSI, U-Net Dice rose from 72.59% to 74.55% and nnU-Net Dice from 80.18% to 81.97%, with 6.4% fewer parameters. It was also evaluated on TN3K, UDIAT, BUS-UCLM and CAMUS.
