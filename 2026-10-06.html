Dual-Rate Force-Image Control with Model-Based Orientation Limits for Robotic Ultrasound

Foster, Zhang, Haque, Nguyen · arXiv, 5 Oct 2026 · 8-page conference paper

The force loop in robotic ultrasound runs fast, but image feedback arrives slowly and with a delay. The authors derive a closed-form limit on how fast the probe may rotate so that the force disturbance stays bounded until the image loop can respond. The limit depends on contact stiffness, control gains and the prediction horizon. They reconstruct the delay from timestamps and sense force from joint torques. On gelatin phantoms, the limit cut RMS force error in the first second by 0.40 N, at the cost of 0.94 s longer convergence. A plain fixed rate cap did about as well, so the authors conclude that the model's main value is as a rule for choosing the rate. A feasibility test on human tissue was limited by image noise.

Why it matters: this is a clean analysis of a slow, delayed loop interacting with a fast contact loop, the same structure as delayed image and force feedback in bilateral tele-ultrasound.

Recon2Servo: Robotic Ultrasound Visual Servoing via Learned Image-to-Motion Inference

Zhang, Liu, Huang, Qian, Chen, Chu, Au, Jiang · arXiv, 5 Oct 2026

A DINOv3 encoder fine-tuned with LoRA estimates the relative 6-DoF pose between the current and a target B-mode image. This addresses the ambiguity of out-of-plane motion in 2D images. The framework combines supervised relative-pose learning, closed-loop adaptation guided by reconstruction, and a bounded residual pose correction. It was evaluated on public datasets and on data from 12 healthy volunteers acquired with several ultrasound systems. Real-robot demonstrations covered aligning to a target view and tracking on human forearms. The abstract gives no headline accuracy numbers.

Why it matters: image-to-pose inference like this could give novice operators “move toward the target view” cues in human tele-ultrasound, not only drive a robot.

Now You Feel It, Now You See Me: Digital-Twin-based Teleoperation Interface for Dexterous Manipulation

Shim, Lee, Kim, Hwang, Ji, Lee · arXiv, 4 Oct 2026

This AR interface for bare-hand teleoperation of bimanual robot hands has two features. It renders tactile measurements as colour on the virtual robot hands (“visuo-force feedback”), and it reduces occlusion by letting the operator pick virtual viewpoints and render parts of the scene transparent. User studies tested a force-precision task and a task with the view blocked. The abstract does not report numbers.

Why it matters: showing force visually in place of haptic actuators is a design choice also open to MR tele-ultrasound guidance.

DASH: A da Vinci Adapter for Serial-link and Humanoid Robots as an Accessible Platform for Surgical Robotics Research

Wickenhiser, Zhou, Liang, Peiros, Yip (UCSD) · arXiv, 5 Oct 2026

DASH is a pair of adapters that mount unmodified da Vinci Classic and Xi instruments on general-purpose arms and humanoids. They use the instrument's own latch. Each adapter identifies the inserted tool wirelessly and loads that instrument's kinematics and coupling matrices automatically. The system was validated in teleoperated experiments on several robots with different payloads.

Why it matters: this offers a low-cost route to a teleoperated surgical testbed on lab arms you may already have.

TacGELLO: Tactile Contact Feedback for a 3D-Printed Teleoperation Leader

Kim, Song, Kodagoda, Carmichael, Thiyagarajan · arXiv, 4 Oct 2026

TacGELLO retrofits the passive GELLO leader arm with feedback, using the servos it already has. When AnySkin sensors on the follower detect contact, the leader's gripper is held closed. Joint currents on the follower set servo limits on the leader, so that lifting feels resisted. Command tracking error was 0.15–0.36° RMSE, with an estimated lag of 82–98 ms. Contact was detected in 60 of 62 grasps, and gripper over-closure fell from about 13 mm to 0.7–2.1 mm. The tests used a UR3 and a single operator.

Industry & news

Mendaera's Focalist System Now Compatible with Butterfly's Ultrasound-on-Chip Technology

Press release via BioPharmaWatch · 1 Oct 2026

Mendaera's FDA-cleared Focalist is a handheld robotic system with guidance software for needle procedures such as vascular access, nerve blocks and organ access. It is now commercially available with Butterfly's ultrasound-on-chip imaging built in. This is the first “Butterfly Embedded” partner product to reach market. Both companies are exhibiting at ACEP26 in Chicago, 5–7 Oct. Yesterday this story was listed under Other links because the MPO page was blocked; today it has been verified from this copy.

Why it matters: Butterfly's probe is turning into a programmable imaging platform for third-party handheld robots, which could serve as a building block for low-cost guided or tele-ultrasound systems.
