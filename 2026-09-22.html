Top picks

AWR-Net: Decoupling Anatomy and Appearance for 3D Fetal Brain Ultrasound Synthesis

Lu, Valencia, Zhang, Huang, Jaimes, Grant, Karimi · arXiv 2609.22635, 18 Sep 2026

Uses wavelet diffusion plus residual refinement to synthesize realistic 3D fetal brain ultrasound volumes from anatomical label maps, borrowing richer anatomy from fetal MRI datasets to compensate for ultrasound's more limited labeled data. Improves downstream segmentation, especially on abnormal cases.

Why it matters: a decoupled anatomy/appearance synthesis approach for 3D ultrasound reconstruction/segmentation training data, relevant to your work generating and reconstructing 3D volumes from sparse or freehand scans.

Compression Hurts, Pooling Helps: Information Loss in Rayleigh-Scale Estimation from B-Mode Ultrasound

D. Hudson Smith, Ahmer Raza · arXiv 2609.19525, 17 Sep 2026

A Fisher-information analysis showing that the log-compression baked into clinical B-mode images sharply raises the minimum achievable variance when estimating the Rayleigh scale parameter (a quantitative tissue-characterization statistic), and that pooling across image windows only partially recovers the lost information.

Why it matters: a fundamental-limits result on how much quantitative tissue information survives standard B-mode display pipelines — relevant to any downstream elastography/tissue-characterization work built on routine clinical images.

Task-Relevant Feature-Dynamics Fidelity Enables Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer for Robotic Ultrasound Scanning

Qian, Luo, Zhu, Zhang, Meng, Yuan, Liu · arXiv 2608.29516, 30 Aug 2026

Argues that zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for probe-motion policies depends less on visual realism of the simulator than on whether simulated observations reproduce the same task-relevant feature changes induced by probe motion (\"TR-FDF\"). A policy trained purely in simulation with this property succeeded in 390/400 real-world scanning trials across different planes.

Why it matters: a concrete, testable criterion for what a robotic-ultrasound simulator needs to get right to support sim-to-real learning of scanning policies.
