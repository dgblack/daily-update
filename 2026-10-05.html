Top picks

Remote robotic urological surgery with the Toumai System across 25–7,000 km: a systematic review and single-arm meta-analysis

Cao, Li, Qin, … Yang · Journal of Robotic Surgery, Oct 2026 (PMID 42821197) · abstract read via Europe PMC because the Springer page redirects to a login

The review pools 95 remote urological procedures done on the Toumai system over distances of 25 to 7,000 km. All 95 were technically successful, and none of the 85 evaluable cases had a Clavien-Dindo grade III or higher complication. Mean latency was 72.8 ms and mean operative time was 145 min. The authors rate the overall certainty of evidence as low.

Why it matters: this gives a single figure for real clinical latency, about 70 ms, to cite as a baseline when motivating delay compensation or model-mediated tele-ultrasound.

Contact-Aware Impedance Controller for Robot-Assisted Ultrasound Imaging

Arefin, Tiryaki · arXiv, 28 Sep 2026 · submitted to ICRA 2027

The controller detects acoustic contact directly from B-mode images rather than from force measurements. This lets it re-zero (tare) the force/torque sensor whenever contact is detected, removing measurement bias. It also tracks where along the curved probe face the contact sits and shifts the impedance control frame to match. On an agar phantom, contact was localised to 1.46 ± 0.14 mm across probe tilts of ±15°. Peak compressive force dropped by 36%, from 31.6 N to 20.1 N, with tracking accuracy unchanged.

Why it matters: image-based contact estimation could feed the patient and tissue model in model-mediated tele-ultrasound, or act as a safety layer on the follower robot.

Embodiment-aware control by inference over the operator: a simulation study

Sara Falcone · arXiv, 29 Sep 2026 · code and data on GitHub/Zenodo

The paper proposes the “Universal Embodiment Engine”, an active-inference controller. It infers an operator's sense of embodiment and sensory preferences from gaze, pupil and performance signals, then adjusts the teleoperation device settings to suit. Across 300 simulated operators, it found suitable settings within about 30 s for most of them. Embodiment scores were 1.68 against 0.73 for fixed settings. Naive prediction-error minimisation backfired: it reduced feedback, and task success fell from 0.90 to 0.74.

Why it matters: this is an approach for automatically personalising haptic and visual feedback per operator in human teleoperation.

Scanning While Imagining: A Scene-Graph World Model for Robotic Ultrasound Navigation

Li, Chen, Li, Jiang, Navab, Bi (TUM) · arXiv, 26 Sep 2026

SonoGraph-WM does not synthesise future ultrasound images. Instead, a Transformer predicts future anatomical scene graphs (which structures are visible and how they relate spatially) together with probe poses. It is trained on data generated from CT. Spatial-relation F1 stays above 93% over 20 prediction steps. Navigation success was 77.5% for gallbladder targets and 75.0% for pancreas targets.

DITTO-X: Forward and Reverse Teleoperation for Dexterous Manipulation and Human Intervention

He, Palacios, Wang, et al. · arXiv, 30 Sep 2026

DITTO-X is a dexterous-hand teleoperation interface that works with several robot hands (Sharpa, Wuji and Inspire) without being redesigned for each. It renders joint-level force and fingertip contact events using sensors already on the hand. In “reverse teleoperation”, the robot first moves the operator's fingers into the robot's current pose and only then hands over control, so the operator can take over mid-task without disorientation. It collected better demonstrations than commercial tracking gloves on contact-rich tasks.

Why it matters: reverse teleoperation is a concrete design for the handover between autonomous and human control, relevant to shared-autonomy ultrasound.

Ultrasound & robotic ultrasound

Contact-Adaptive Robotic Ultrasound Probe Control for Tissue Exploration and Continuous Task-Relevant Visualization Using Robot-Free Image-Motion

Jeong, Kim, Yee, et al. · arXiv, 27 Sep 2026 · submitted to ICRA 2027

“Sonologger” attaches an IMU to a clinician's handheld probe and records which probe axis and direction they use to correct each image offset. No robot or external tracking is needed for this. Those recordings parameterise an interpretable, non-learned image-motion controller that keeps a clinician-selected view on screen while the tissue moves. On a bladder phantom it held the target view in 7 of 7 trials, against 1 of 7 for static holding (p = 0.031). It also respected force limits during a simulated fluid injection.

Why it matters: this is a cheap way to capture sonographer correction strategies, which could inform the guidance cues given to novice operators in human teleoperation.

Uncertainty Quantification in Cardiac Model Personalisation from Ultrafast Ultrasound

Ferrario, Venet, Villemain, Sermesant (Inria) · arXiv, 28 Sep 2026

The authors use simulation-based inference with neural posterior estimation to fit a 0D cardiovascular model to myocardial stiffness curves from shear-wave elastography. It estimates three parameters: active stiffness scale, contraction rate and relaxation rate. In six healthy volunteers, the stiffness error fell from 12.6 to 1.1 kPa. The authors stress running diagnostic checks on whether a patient's data fits the model's assumptions.

Open-Source Live-Reconfigurable Multi-Mode Wearable Ultrasound

Hirschi, Villani, Benini, Cossettini (ETH Zürich) · arXiv, 1 Oct 2026

An open-source control stack for the TinyProbe wearable platform cuts the time to switch modes from 30.2 ms to 3.1 ms when scheduled on the microcontroller. That allows pulsed-wave Doppler and A-mode imaging to be interleaved at 16 Hz, 90% of the theoretical maximum. The same group posted companion preprints on an 8-channel transmit IP for RISC-V SoCs and a multi-wire SPI readout; these were seen in the listing only.

Industry & news

“Robotics to expand beyond surgery into wider medical care”: Jitendra Singh

Daijiworld (IANS) · 3 Oct 2026

India's Minister of State for Science and Technology spoke at the opening of the Society of Robotic Surgery's international conference. He said he had performed a robotic ultrasound on a person about 10,000 km away in Antarctica, and argued for extending tele-robotics into diagnostics.

Why it matters: India's government is now publicly backing robotic tele-ultrasound, alongside its telesurgery push.
