Top picks

Beyond Pose Control: Anatomically Consistent Ultrasound Video Generation

Yue Zhou, Fangxu Han, Tony Danjun Wang, Nassir Navab, Yuan Bi · arXiv cs.CV, 25 Nov 2026

Pose-conditioned ultrasound generators can produce implausible anatomy in regions that were never scanned and different anatomy when a region is revisited. The authors fix this with a population anatomical prior that is refined online toward the subject, plus a 3D latent memory that collects generated slices into a patient volume that can be resliced at new poses. They tested on thyroid, kidney and lumbar spine and report gains over baselines, but the abstract gives no numbers.

Why it matters: a consistent, pose-driven ultrasound generator is the kind of patient model that model-mediated tele-ultrasound and scan simulators need.

HoloSplat: Real-Time Token Streaming for Feed-Forward Human Reconstruction

Chaofan Li, Qihan Ding, Shihan Wang, et al. (Lan Xu, Xin Lou) · arXiv cs.CV, 23 Nov 2026

HoloSplat streams human volumetric video as compressed Gaussian tokens. The server prunes and compresses the tokens, and the client restores them. It runs at 32.2 FPS on four RTX 5090s at 50 Mbit/s and decodes in 22.1 ms per frame on a laptop RTX 3050 Ti. It sends 22.8× less data than uncompressed float32 Gaussians, and online calibration adds 1.05 dB PSNR.

Why it matters: it gives bandwidth and latency figures for sending a live 3D avatar of the patient or operator in a mixed-reality tele-ultrasound session.

Three-Dimensional Ultrasound Localization Microscopy Using Hadamard-Encoded Multipulse and 4D Denoising on a 256-Channel System with a 1024-element Matrix Array: A Feasibility Study in Human Kidney Transplants

U-Wai Lok, Jingke Zhang, Jingyi Yin, et al. (17 authors) · arXiv, 19 Nov 2026

The authors do 3D ultrasound localization microscopy on a 256-channel research system. Hadamard-encoded multipulse transmission improved microbubble contrast by about 2 dB in phantoms, and 4D non-local-means denoising raised contrast by up to about 9 dB in four human kidney transplants. Resolution was about 84 µm, and 3000 volumes (about 12 s) reached about 74% of the estimated saturated vessel density.

Teleoperation & mixed reality

Design, Control, and Teleoperation of a Dexterous Robotic Hand with Soft-Rigid Hybrid Fingers

Chao Liu, Wil J. Norton, Branden Romero, Daniela Rus · arXiv cs.RO, 24 Nov 2026 (v2 25 Nov)

The hand is tendon-driven, with five soft-rigid hybrid fingers and 12 active degrees of freedom. It reaches 28 of 33 (84.8%) human grasp postures, and finger extension runs up to 1432 deg/s against about 700 deg/s for a human joint. The paper also covers its position and task-space controllers and a teleoperation framework.

Ultrasound & robotic ultrasound

UPP-Gauss: Ultrasound Propagation Physics-informed Gaussian Splatting for B-mode Novel-View Synthesis

Guil Jung, Seok-Hwan Oh, Myeong-Gee Kim, et al. (Hyeon-Min Bae, KAIST) · arXiv cs.CV, 25 Nov 2026

UPP-Gauss renders B-mode images at new probe poses. It samples three 3D Gaussian fields according to the probe pose and beam direction, then combines them using a model of ultrasound propagation. On three datasets it improved PSNR over the best baseline by an average of 1.43 dB.

Why it matters: like the top pick, it is a way to fill in views the operator has not scanned yet from tracked sweeps.

Computer vision & imaging

SalivaryHSI-Seg: A New Challenging Benchmark for Ex Vivo Salivary Gland Hyperspectral Tissue Segmentation

Xiaoqi Guo, Xianran Wang, Xinquan Yang, et al. · arXiv cs.CV, 23 Nov 2026

The benchmark has 119 hyperspectral samples from 31 patients, with pixel-level multi-class labels and patient-level five-fold cross-validation. The authors' boundary-guided spectral-difference network, HyBSD, reaches 74.97% mDice and 87.76% tumour Dice, and full-cube input beats sampled spectra. They say the dataset and code will be released.

Why it matters: it is a new public, patient-split HSI tissue-segmentation benchmark, which could be compared with brain-tumour HSI work.

Recent progress in biomarker-responsive fluorescent probes for real-time monitoring of glioblastoma

Li B, Li Y, Li Q, et al. · Analyst, epub 25 Nov 2026 · doi:10.1039/d6an01251e

The review covers glioblastoma fluorescent probes since 2023 that respond to biomarkers. The authors conclude that the probes could make fluorescence-guided surgery more precise, but only after margin-level validation in clinical GBM models, which they say is still missing.
